      How these numbers are made
      • Scale: the person mask’s head-to-heel span in the front frame equals your height, giving centimetres per pixel at your body plane.
      • Levels: pose landmarks place the neck (mid-neck), chest (27% down the torso), waist (narrowest), hip (fullest, near the hip joints), thigh (3 cm below the crotch), knee, calf and ankle.
      • Turntable girths: while you turn with arms out, each frame gives the torso’s and each leg’s caliper width from one angle. A tape follows the convex outline, whose perimeter is the integral of those widths over half a turn (Cauchy’s formula), so no ellipse is assumed. Each leg uses its own landmarks to correct for perspective as it swings nearer or farther.
      • Two-view girths: front width and profile depth, with an elliptical cross-section.
      • ± is a design estimate per method, not a validated accuracy. Clothing, hair, posture and camera tilt dominate real-world error.
